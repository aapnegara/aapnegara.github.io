Why it matters

[draft] Stars of low and intermediate mass end their lives on the asymptotic giant branch, where slow, dense winds return processed material to the interstellar medium. These outflows are rich chemical laboratories in which molecules and dust grains form, so understanding them links stellar evolution to the chemical enrichment of galaxies.

Why it matters

[draft] How much mass a massive star loses as a red supergiant is still uncertain, yet it decides how the star ends its life: what kind of supernova it produces, and what remnant is left behind. Varying the mass-loss rate in models shows how sensitive these outcomes are.
